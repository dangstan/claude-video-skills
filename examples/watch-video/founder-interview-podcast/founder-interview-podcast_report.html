Executive summary

This is a two-person conversational podcast, not a tutorial. David Ondrej interviews Flo Crivello, founder of the AI-teammate product Lindy, about why he believes AI tooling is stuck in a "single-player" phase -- everyone running their own isolated Claude Code or Codex session -- while the real unlock is "multiplayer": agents that share memory, files, skills, and integrations across an entire team the way Slack or Google Docs already let humans collaborate. Crivello demonstrates this through his own product: a memory system that quietly hydrates itself from a team's Slack history and meetings instead of relying on a hand-written wiki, a library of toggleable "routines" that cover his entire personal workflow from daily briefs to recruiting outreach, and an anecdote about an agent that wrote its own API integration rather than waiting to be given one. The conversation widens into a founder's playbook for operating under fast model iteration (embrace chaos, spend 20 hours a week hands-on, expect to break things) and a philosophical stretch on AGI risk, infrastructure investment, and where human judgment still matters. A paid sponsor segment for a web-agent API product (DeepAPI) sits in the middle of the recording.

The idea, in the medium it's actually about

The video's central claim is that Slack -- not a new app -- becomes the place AI agents collaborate with a team, because an agent invoked inside an existing thread inherits everything already said there. What to build: "how" over "what," then infrastructure over application layer

Argues indie/lifestyle businesses succeed on execution speed, not idea novelty (his example: a solo developer allegedly at "seven or eight million ARR" via a save-commit-push-deploy loop). For VC-scale bets, advises asking whether the business still matters in a world where humans barely operate businesses -- concluding infrastructure (compute, hardware, bio) is the healthier long-term category versus software applications.
